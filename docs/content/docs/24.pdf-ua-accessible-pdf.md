An accessible PDF is a tagged PDF: next to the painted pages, it holds a
structure tree that tells assistive technology, such as a screen reader, which
text is a heading, a paragraph, a list, or a table, in which order to read it,
and what each image shows. PDF/UA-1 (ISO 14289-1) is the standard for
accessible PDF, and PDF/A-3a is the archival PDF/A level that is also tagged.
Public bodies, accessibility laws, and some archives ask for them; check which
standard your recipient names.

Nuxt PDF writes the structure tree from your template. You mark headings,
describe images, and group content; the render checks the rules that it can
check and fails with a typed error when the document cannot meet the claim.

## Make a document accessible

```vue [pdfs/welcome.vue]
<script setup lang="ts">
definePdf({ title: 'Welcome to Fieldnote', language: 'en' })
</script>

<template>
  <PdfDocument conformance="PDF/UA-1">
    <PdfPage size="A4" :style="{ fontFamily: 'Brand Sans', padding: 48 }">
      <PdfText tag="h1">Welcome to Fieldnote</PdfText>
      <PdfText>Your account is ready. This letter explains the next steps.</PdfText>
      <PdfImage src="team.jpg" alt="The Fieldnote team in front of the Vienna studio" />
    </PdfPage>
  </PdfDocument>
</template>
```

1. Register a font in `pdf.fonts` and set `fontFamily` on every page. A tagged
   document requires embedded fonts, so the standard PDF fonts (Helvetica,
   Times, and Courier) fail the render. See
   [Register a local font]($docs.images-and-fonts).
2. Set `title` and `language` on `PdfDocument` or in `definePdf`. Viewers
   show the title in place of the file name, and screen readers use the
   language to pronounce the text. The language is a language tag such as
   `en` or `de-CH`.
3. Set `conformance` on `PdfDocument`.
4. Mark each heading with `tag` on its `PdfText`.
5. Give each `PdfImage` and `PdfSvg` an `alt` text, or mark it `decorative`.

Paragraphs, tables, and links need no extra props. See
[What is tagged without props](#what-is-tagged-without-props).

## Conformance levels

| `conformance` | Writes | Requires |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `'PDF/UA-1'` | A tagged PDF that claims PDF/UA-1. | `title`, `language`, embedded fonts, `alt` or `decorative` on images. |
| `'PDF/A-3a'` | A tagged archival PDF/A-3 document (conformance A). | `language`, embedded fonts, `alt` or `decorative` on images. |
| `'PDF/A-3b'` | An archival PDF/A-3 document without tags. See [PDF/A and Factur-X]($docs.pdf-a-and-factur-x). | Embedded fonts. |

To claim more than one standard, pass a list. A list holds at most one PDF/A
level:

```vue
<PdfDocument title="Annual report 2025" language="en" :conformance="['PDF/A-3a', 'PDF/UA-1']">
  <PdfPage :style="{ fontFamily: 'Brand Sans' }">
    <PdfText tag="h1">Annual report 2025</PdfText>
  </PdfPage>
</PdfDocument>
```

Use `['PDF/A-3a', 'PDF/UA-1']` for a document that must be both archival and
accessible. `['PDF/A-3b', 'PDF/UA-1']` is also valid. Every level writes PDF
1.7, so `pdfVersion` must be absent or `'1.7'`.

A `PdfFacturX` invoice is PDF/A-3b without `conformance`. With
`['PDF/A-3a', 'PDF/UA-1']` the invoice is also tagged. The repository
validates this combination with veraPDF.

Without `'PDF/UA-1'` or `'PDF/A-3a'`, the props `tag`, `alt`, `decorative`,
and `header` change nothing, and the PDF keeps the same bytes. You can add
them to a template before you claim a level.

## Headings

Set `tag` on a page-flow `PdfText` to `'h1'` through `'h6'`. The default is
`'p'`, a paragraph.

```vue
<PdfText tag="h1">Annual report 2025</PdfText>
<PdfText tag="h2">Results</PdfText>
<PdfText tag="h3">Revenue by quarter</PdfText>
<PdfText>Revenue grew in every quarter.</PdfText>
```

Headings follow these rules:

- The first heading of the document is `h1`.
- A heading goes at most one level deeper than the heading before it: `h2`
  after `h1`, not `h3` after `h1`. A heading can go back to any higher level.
- A heading inside a `fixed` or `decorative` node does not count, because the
  node is an artifact.
- `tag` is not allowed on a `PdfText` nested inside another `PdfText`. The
  nested text is part of its parent.

`tag` sets the role only. The look comes from your styles, so a small label
can be a heading and a large number can be a paragraph.

## Images and graphics

A tagged document requires `alt` or `decorative` on every `PdfImage` and
`PdfSvg`, also on an image inside a `PdfText`. An image inside a `figure`
view takes the `alt` text of the figure; see
[Figures with a caption](#figures-with-a-caption).

- `alt` is a short text that says what the image shows. For a chart, write
  what the chart shows: its values, or its main result if the values are
  printed next to it.
- `decorative` marks an image that carries no information, such as an
  ornament or a background shape. Assistive technology skips it.

```vue
<PdfSvg :alt="`Revenue by quarter: ${quarterSummary}`" viewBox="0 0 400 200">
  <PdfRect v-for="bar in bars" :key="bar.id" :x="bar.x" :y="bar.y" width="30" :height="bar.height" fill="#2F5D3A" />
</PdfSvg>
<PdfImage src="ornament.png" decorative />
```

The shapes inside a `PdfSvg`, and a `PdfImage` inside a `PdfSvg`, are part of
the graphic and need no `alt` of their own.

## Figures with a caption

A chart is often more than one image: a caption, the graphic, and labels
around it. Set `tag="figure"` on the `PdfView` that holds them, and give the
view the `alt` text. The view becomes one figure (`Figure`) with that text:

```vue
<PdfText tag="h3">Revenue by quarter</PdfText>
<PdfView tag="figure" :alt="`Revenue by quarter, in EUR thousands: ${quarterSummary}`">
  <PdfView tag="caption">
    <PdfText>Figure 2: consulting and licensing, EUR thousands</PdfText>
  </PdfView>
  <PdfSvg viewBox="0 0 400 200">
    <PdfRect v-for="bar in bars" :key="bar.id" :x="bar.x" :y="bar.y" width="30" :height="bar.height" fill="#2F5D3A" />
  </PdfSvg>
  <PdfText v-for="tick in ticks" :key="tick.value">{{ tick.label }}</PdfText>
</PdfView>
```

- The images, graphics, and texts inside the figure are its content.
  Assistive technology reads the `alt` text of the figure in their place, so
  they need no `alt` of their own.
- A `PdfView tag="caption"` inside the figure becomes the caption (`Caption`)
  of the figure. Its content has the usual structure, so it can hold a link.
- Keep the heading outside the figure. A heading, a list, a table, or a link
  inside the figure, outside its caption, fails the render.
- Write the `alt` text for everything that the figure shows. Labels and a
  legend inside the figure are part of it, so put their values into the
  `alt` text.
- `decorative` on the figure makes the figure, its caption included, an
  artifact. A figure without `alt` and without `decorative` fails the render.

`decorative` is accepted on every primitive with the shared base props, on
page-flow `PdfText`, and on `PdfSvg`. The node and everything inside it
become an artifact: content that assistive technology skips. Use it, for
example, for axis labels when the `alt` text of the chart already holds the
values.

## What is tagged without props

| Template | Structure in the PDF |
| --- | --- |
| Page-flow `PdfText` | A paragraph (`P`), or a heading with `tag`. Nested `PdfText`, inline links, and inline images are part of the paragraph. |
| A page token in a page-flow `PdfText` | Part of its paragraph. |
| `PdfImage`, `PdfSvg` with `alt` | A figure (`Figure`) with the alt text. Inside a `PdfText`, the figure sits inside the paragraph. |
| `PdfTable` | A table (`Table`). |
| `PdfTableHeader` | A row (`TR`) of header cells (`TH`) that apply to their column. |
| `PdfTableRow` | A row (`TR`) of data cells (`TD`). |
| `colspan` on `PdfTableCell` | The cell spans the same number of columns in the structure. |
| `header` on a `PdfTableCell` in a `PdfTableRow` | A header cell (`TH`) that applies to its row. In a `PdfTableHeader` the cells stay column headers. |
| A `PdfText` with `textTransform` or `letterSpacing` | The text in the template, as `ActualText`, next to the painted capitals or spaced letters. |
| `PdfLink` | A link (`Link`) that holds the link text and the clickable area. A link inside a `PdfText` splits the paragraph: text, link, text. Each `PdfLink` is its own link, also next to a link with the same `href`. |
| `PdfView` without `tag` | No element. Its content belongs to the nearest element above it. |
| Backgrounds and borders of pages and views | Artifacts. |

A cell that holds only text has that text as its content. A cell that holds
several texts or other elements holds them as paragraphs and figures.

Mark the cells that name their row with `header`, such as the first column of
a ledger:

```vue
<PdfTableRow v-for="row in ledger" :key="row.id">
  <PdfTableCell header>{{ row.label }}</PdfTableCell>
  <PdfTableCell align="right">{{ row.current }}</PdfTableCell>
</PdfTableRow>
```

`textTransform: 'uppercase'` paints capital letters, and a wide
`letterSpacing` makes some text tools read single letters. A tagged document
keeps the text of the template next to the painted text as `ActualText`, so
assistive technology reads "From the managing director", not "FROM THE
MANAGING DIRECTOR". A text that continues on the next page carries the source
of its own part on each page.

Assistive technology reads a link by its description. The description is the
link text and the alt texts of images inside the link. A link without text
and without alt text uses its `href`.

A link is inline content: it can hold texts and images, but no headings,
sections, lists, quotes, captions, figures or tables. To make the items of a
list clickable, put a `PdfLink` inside each item, not around the list:

```vue
<PdfView tag="list">
  <PdfView v-for="item in items" :key="item.href" tag="list-item">
    <PdfLink :href="item.href">{{ item.title }}</PdfLink>
  </PdfView>
</PdfView>
```

## Group content

Set `tag` on a `PdfView` to give a group a role:

| `tag` | Structure | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `'section'` | `Sect` | A part of the document with its own heading. |
| `'list'` | `L` | A list. |
| `'list-item'` | `LI` with its content in `LBody` | One item of a list. |
| `'quote'` | `BlockQuote` | A quotation of one or more paragraphs. |
| `'caption'` | `Caption` | The caption of a figure. Inside a `figure` view it belongs to the figure. |
| `'figure'` | `Figure` with the `alt` text | A figure made of several nodes. See [Figures with a caption](#figures-with-a-caption). |

```vue
<PdfView tag="section">
  <PdfText tag="h2">Next steps</PdfText>
  <PdfView tag="list">
    <PdfView v-for="(step, index) in steps" :key="step.id" tag="list-item">
      <PdfText>{{ index + 1 }}. {{ step.title }}</PdfText>
    </PdfView>
  </PdfView>
</PdfView>
```

Lists follow these rules:

- A `list-item` is a direct child of a `list`.
- A `list` holds only `list-item` and `caption` views. Content that is
  `fixed` or `decorative` is also allowed.
- The bullet or number that you print stays inside the item content. There is
  no separate label element.

Put a figure and its caption into a `figure` view:

```vue
<PdfView tag="figure" alt="Map of the 14 survey sites along the Danube">
  <PdfImage src="map.png" />
  <PdfView tag="caption">
    <PdfText>Figure 1: survey sites in 2025</PdfText>
  </PdfView>
</PdfView>
```

A `caption` view outside a `figure` view is a caption next to the content
before it, not a part of it.

## Artifacts

An artifact is painted content outside the structure tree. Assistive
technology skips it. These are artifacts:

- `fixed` content, such as running heads, footers, and page numbers in them;
- `decorative` content;
- backgrounds and borders of pages and views;
- the repeats of a table header: the header is tagged on the page where the
  table starts, and its copies on later pages are artifacts;
- the boxes and size labels of `debug` and of the preview's layout boxes.

Do not put information only into a `fixed` node. A reader who uses assistive
technology does not hear it.

## Reading order

Assistive technology reads the content in template order, not in the order of
positions on the page. An absolutely positioned node is read where it stands in
the template, and a row of views is read from the first child to the last.
Write the template in the order in which a reader should hear the content.

## Not supported in a tagged document

- `PdfNote` fails the render. A note is a pop-up outside the page content, so
  a reader who cannot open it misses its text, and the engine writes it
  without an appearance. Print the note as `PdfText`.
- A `PdfLink` inside a `fixed` or `decorative` node fails the render. Every
  link must be in the reading order, and running content is an artifact. Move
  the link into the page flow.
- A `PdfTableHeader` with `pages` is an artifact, so the table has no header
  row in its structure. Keep `pages` off table headers in a tagged document.
- `ActualText` is not written for a text that holds an inline `PdfLink` or
  an inline `PdfImage`. Such a text reads with its painted capitals.
- `ActualText` is not written for the part of a text where the template text
  cannot be found exactly: `'ß'` becomes `'SS'` in capitals, so a page break
  inside a text with `ß` leaves the parts of that text without it. A text that
  `maxLines` cuts off has no `ActualText` from the cut on.
- Table captions: a `caption` view next to a table is not a part of the table.

## Failures

Each of these fails the render with `PDF_TREE_INVALID` and the source position
of the node. The messages name the claimed standard: `PDF/UA-1`, `PDF/A-3a`,
or `PDF/A-3a and PDF/UA-1`.

| Cause | Message | Inspect or change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No `title` with PDF/UA-1 | `<PdfDocument> claims PDF/UA-1, which requires a "title": viewers show it in place of the file name.` | Set `title` on `PdfDocument`. |
| No `language` | `<PdfDocument> claims PDF/UA-1, which requires a "language", such as "en" or "de-CH".` | Set `language` on `PdfDocument`. |
| A `language` that is not a language tag | `<PdfDocument> claims PDF/UA-1, which requires "language" to be a language tag such as "en" or "de-CH"; "de_DE" is not one. Write "de-DE".` | Write a BCP 47 language tag: subtags joined with `-`, not `_`. |
| An image without `alt` | `<PdfImage> needs "alt" (a short text that says what it shows) or "decorative" in a PDF/UA-1 document.` | Add `alt`, or `decorative` when the image carries no information. The same message names `<PdfSvg>`. |
| An empty `alt` | `<PdfImage> requires "alt" to be a non-empty string.` | Write the text, or use `decorative`. |
| The first heading is not `h1` | `<PdfText tag="h2"> is the first heading of the document; a PDF/UA-1 document starts with tag="h1".` | Make the first heading `h1`. |
| A skipped heading level | `<PdfText tag="h4"> skips a heading level: the heading before it is h2. Use tag="h3".` | Use the level that the message names. |
| A text in a standard font | `PDF/UA-1 requires embedded fonts, but <PdfText> uses the standard font "Helvetica", …` | Register a font and set `fontFamily` on the page. |
| A list item outside a list | `<PdfView tag="list-item"> must be a direct child of <PdfView tag="list">.` | Move the item into a `list`, or remove its `tag`. |
| Other content in a list | `<PdfView tag="list"> holds <PdfView tag="list-item"> and <PdfView tag="caption"> elements only; wrap this <PdfText> in a list item.` | Wrap the named node in a `list-item`. |
| A link in running content | `<PdfLink> inside a fixed or decorative node is not supported in a PDF/UA-1 document: …` | Move the link into the page flow. |
| A `PdfNote` | `<PdfNote> is not supported in a PDF/UA-1 document: …` | Print the note as `PdfText`. With a PDF/A level, the PDF/A message for notes comes first. |
| An unknown or repeated level, an empty list, or two PDF/A levels | `<PdfDocument> requires "conformance" to be "PDF/A-3b", "PDF/A-3a" or "PDF/UA-1", or a list of them with at most one PDF/A level.` | Pass one level, or a list with at most one PDF/A level. |
| `pdfVersion` other than `'1.7'` | `<PdfDocument> claims PDF/A-3a and PDF/UA-1, which is written as PDF 1.7; remove "pdfVersion" or set it to "1.7".` | Remove `pdfVersion`. |
| An unknown text `tag` | `<PdfText> requires "tag" to be "h1", "h2", "h3", "h4", "h5", "h6" or "p".` | Use one of the listed values. |
| An unknown view `tag` | `<PdfView> requires "tag" to be "section", "list", "list-item", "quote", "caption" or "figure".` | Use one of the listed values. |
| A figure without `alt` | `<PdfView tag="figure"> needs "alt" (a short text that says what the figure shows) or "decorative" in a PDF/UA-1 document.` | Add `alt` to the figure view, or `decorative`. |
| Structure inside a link | `<PdfView tag="list"> inside <PdfLink> is not supported in a PDF/UA-1 document: a link is inline content, so it cannot hold headings, sections, lists, quotes, captions, figures or tables, and their structure would be lost. …` | Put the link inside the blocks, for example around the text of each list item. The same message names a `PdfTable`, or another `PdfText` or `PdfView` with `tag`. |
| Structure inside a figure | `<PdfLink> is not supported inside <PdfView tag="figure">: assistive technology reads the figure as its "alt", so the content inside it has no structure of its own. …` | Move the named node out of the figure or into its caption. The same message names a `PdfTable`, or a `PdfText` or `PdfView` with `tag`. |
| `alt` on a view without `tag="figure"` | `"alt" on <PdfView> needs tag="figure": a plain view has no alternative text.` | Set `tag="figure"`, or move `alt` to the image. This check also runs without a tagged level. |
| `tag` on a nested text | `"tag" is not supported on a <PdfText> nested inside another <PdfText>: the nested text is part of its parent.` | Move `tag` to the outer `PdfText`. |

After an update of the layout engine, the render can also fail with
`PDF_RENDER_ERROR`: `Tagged PDF: the engine painted an annotation the
structure plan did not expect; …`. This is a defect in Nuxt PDF, not in your
template. Report it with the template that fails.

## Evidence and limits

The repository validates tagged output with veraPDF, the reference validator
for PDF/A and PDF/UA, with its PDF/UA-1, PDF/A-3a, and PDF/A-3b profiles. The
test documents pass with headings, paragraphs that continue on the next page,
figures, decorative images, running heads, a table over two pages with a
repeated header and a spanned cell, external and internal links, a list, a
section, a quotation, a caption, a figure view with its caption, row headers,
and `ActualText` on capitals and letter spacing. The annual report in the
[examples]($docs.examples) passes the PDF/UA-1 and PDF/A-3a profiles.

veraPDF checks the rules that a machine can check. PDF/UA-1 also has rules
that a person must judge:

- each alt text says what its image shows;
- each link text makes sense without the text around it;
- the reading order matches the meaning of the document;
- the headings match the structure of the content.

Check these in your own documents, and open them with a screen reader before
you publish them. Nuxt PDF does not claim:

- that a screen reader reads its output as intended: no screen reader was
  tested, and some text tools, such as pdf.js and poppler, ignore
  `ActualText`;
- a result in other accessibility checkers, such as the one in Adobe Acrobat;
- PDF/UA-2 or WCAG conformance.
